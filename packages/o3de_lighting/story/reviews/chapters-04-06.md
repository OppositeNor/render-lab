# Review: chapters 4, 5, 6

## Chapter 4 · Decals

**Fit.** On budget. No text overlap. The values panel (b, albedo after, F0 rows) is dense but fits.

1. **c4-colour shows nothing change (highest priority).** Three frames are identical: same splat, same readout (`b (0.820,0.280,0.120)`, `b in ACEScg (0.603,0.316,0.152)`, albedo after = b). The sentences claim a finding (decal colour is not converted, so saturated decals shift in hue), but there is no visible consequence.
   - Add a sweep on a decal colour or saturation parameter: grey `(0.5,0.5,0.5)` to saturated `(0.82,0.28,0.12)`. Show the readout's b and "b in ACEScg" rows converging near grey and diverging when saturated.
   - Better: a split view or second swatch pair, "as blended" vs "as a material would be", beside the splat.
   - Light the two readout rows in turn (`highlight=` on the values rows) as the sentences name them.
   - Rewrite "The readout shows b, the colour the top decal blends in, ... and what a material's colour of the same value would become." It points at three things at once. Split it into two sentences, each with a row lit.
2. **c4-what has little on screen for four sentences.** Each sentence only re-calls the same ApplyDecal formula panel while the camera drifts.
   - "The paint splat and panel grid lie on the floor" needs the "grid" and "splat" labels, which are already there, to be lit while named. Pulse each decal box in turn: `decalHighlight=grid`, then `splat`.
   - "A decal changes the surface before the lighting data is set up" would be clearer with a before/after toggle. Show the floor with decals off, then on (`decalsEnabled=0` then `1`, `blend=1`).
   - "Every light sees the decal's albedo, F zero, and normal": show the same floor under the orbiting light with the decal visibly lit. The orbit is there, but nothing says look at it.
3. **c4-apply is not explained step by step.** The code highlight for the box test is lit, but there is no visual for "moves the point into the box" or "skips it outside minus one to one".
   - Show a sample point: the readout already has `uvw (0.73, 0.27, 0.00)`. Add a marker on the floor that walks across the splat's box edge, so `uvw` goes past 1 and the decal stops.
   - Sentence about v flipped is cheap; either show the texture's orientation (a small texture inset with an arrow) or fold it into the previous sentence.
   - Sentence 3 ("by the map's alpha times opacity and its angle fade") names three factors but lights only `albedo`. Add `param.formula=1` (a) first, then `2`.
   - The normal sentence says "into the grooves between the panels", but the grid decal's normal effect is subtle. Raise the light low (`lightY=0.6`) while it is spoken, or show a normal-visualisation `viewMode`, so the bending is visible.
4. **c4-angle sweep is hard to read.** The tilt does fade the splat (frame 3 shows it clipped), but:
   - Sentence 2 says "gone by about fifty-seven degrees". Sweep decalTilt `0 -> 35 -> 58` slowly, with the formula term `s = smoothstep(.55, 1, N·z)` lit (`param.formula=1` is already there) and the readout row `α·opacity·angle` visibly dropping 1.00 to 0.00. The panel is `values` only at the start of that sentence, so put `panel=values` before the first sentence of the beat.
   - Sentence 1 does not say what the "projection axis" is on screen. The arrows are drawn; name them: "the arrow on each box is that axis".
   - The last sentence ("attenuation-angle setting, one by default") jumps in with the camera still. Say what the setting does physically ("zero turns the fade off") and sweep `decalAngleAttenuation` `1 -> 0` with the tilt held at 58, so the splat reappears.
5. **c4-order.** Culling sentence: "puts decals in the first group of each tile's list, as spheres around their boxes" has no visual. Show a tile and the sphere around a box, or at least a tile-grid overlay in the scene. "Larger key applied later and wins" then goes cleanly to the sweep: change `decalSplatSortKey` 17 -> 15 with `blend`, and tell the viewer what to look at ("watch the overlap at the splat's edge"). The overlap is small and the result is visually minor. Move the splat to overlap the grid more (e.g. scene param for decal position) so the swap is obvious. The final sentence about the forward pass walking the group is a restatement of c4-what's last line. Drop one.
6. **Flow.** The chapter opens well ("Before any light reaches that surface"). c4-colour opens with the colour-space sentence cold; bridge it: "One thing the blend does not do is convert colour." The sources beat is fine. Depth gap: nothing says why decals are per-tile culled (why they are in the light list at all), or what the decal's `half size` and `max draw distance` do. A viewer who knows graphics sees "a decal system" but not the novelty (decals evaluated in the forward pass, before lighting, so every light sees them).

## Chapter 5 · The simple point light

**Fit.** On budget. Problems on screen: in c5-record the floating formula tag `w = (1 - (d²/r²)²)²` sits over the scene top-right and clashes with the "(1 - (d²/r²)²)" curve label drawn in the scene. In c5-shadows the four object labels (sphere, teapot, rotated box, light) overlap the objects and each other, with leader lines crossing; they hide the sphere. In c5-radius at r=6 the scene fills the frame and the "1/d²" and window labels pile on the box.

1. **c5-record carries too much in one beat and the formula never lights up in the readout.** Eight sentences, the beat is 62 s. Key points are scattered:
   - "The window is `w = ...`" (the tagged formula has a lit term `\lit{1}{d^2}` that is not what the sentence names). Light the `d²/r²` term when "the squared ratio" is said, then the outer square.
   - Why a window at all? Inverse square never reaches zero, so the renderer would need to shade every pixel for every light. One extra sentence between sentences 3 and 4: "The inverse square never reaches zero, so O3DE multiplies in a window that does." This is the actual reason, and the "skipped entirely" sentence in c5-radius pays it off.
   - "At half the radius it still keeps eighty eight percent" is lit only by `param.formula=2` on the readout. Move the sample point to half the radius (`d` = r/2) and show `(1 - f²)² = 0.879` with the window row lit.
   - "Six candelas at just under two metres give about one point seven lux, before the window" is abstract. The readout shows `1.667` in `I/max(d²,1e-6)`. Light that row. Define candela once ("intensity per steradian, so it is divided by distance squared to get illuminance").
2. **c5-radius sweep needs more.** "Grow the radius, and the window reaches further" sweeps 2.5 -> 6. The scene camera is the same height, so the floor gradient fills the frame and the labels pile up. Sweep `attenuationRadius` `1.5 -> 6` (start smaller so the dark edge is visible), keep the same camera, and pull the camera back a little at r=6. The "skipped" readout row is the real message; light it (it is `no` throughout). Sweep d across the radius with the sample point (or the radius below the sphere distance) so `skipped = yes` appears.
3. **c5-radius auto radius sentence points at nothing.** "In the automatic mode, the radius is the square root of the light's lumens, weighted by luminance, over 0.1" is spoken over a still. Add a formula tag (`r = \sqrt{\Phi\,\ell}/0.1`) and drive the intensity (`intensity=6 -> 24`) so the radius readout changes, with `attenuationRadius` following automatically. Say what "lumens" is relative to candelas ("4 pi times the candelas"), otherwise the viewer cannot do the computation. Check that claim against `point-light-auto-radius` before adding it.
4. **c5-channels: the two key claims are not visibly demonstrated.**
   - "Give the light a mask of one and the sphere a mask of two, and the sphere stops receiving it." The readout row changes to `0x01 & 0x02 -> skipped`, but the sphere in frames 8 and 9 still looks lit (pale yellow, same as before). If the sphere really drops out, say so by sweeping from 1&1 to 1&2 in view, with the camera nearer the sphere and the sphere labelled. If it does not visibly change, the demo is not showing the claim; check that the scene's channel value reaches the sphere shader.
   - "Lights and meshes carry five bits" has no visual of the bits. Use the formula/values panel to show `0x1F & 0x1F`, then `0x01 & 0x02`; it is there but small. Light that row with each sentence.
   - The skylight/decal sentence and the directional-light finding are the one-line asides that point at nothing (frame 9 shows the same panel). The finding is interesting; give it its own code panel (`directional-channel-return` is a callout but the code panel was replaced). Show the loop code with the `return` line highlighted, and say: "so one mismatched light stops the lights after it". Or cut it to the notes.
5. **c5-shadows is the weakest beat.** A new scene (room) with a camera swing and an orbiting light, and the claim "light reaches every surface that faces it, even behind the boxes" shows nothing at the moment of the sentence. The orbit is only 180 degrees over a beat of 15 s.
   - Put the light so a box is between it and the floor/wall, and say "the floor behind this box is lit anyway". Highlight the region with a callout or by dimming the rest.
   - Fix the label overlap: drop `sphere`/`teapot`/`rotated box` labels in this beat (`labels=0` or the equivalent scene toggle), keep only the light label.
   - Contrast with chapter 11 (cascades) would help: one clause, "unlike the sun, which has shadow cascades later in this video".
   - Last sentence: "With no shadow maps, and culled per screen tile, each one costs little more than shading the pixels inside its radius" is dense, and the callout is the `ENABLE_LIGHT_CULLING` define. Chapter 2 covered culling; refer back: "the tile culling of chapter two keeps the cost to the pixels in its radius".
6. **Flow.** First sentence bridges well ("The surface is ready, decals included"). Between beats: c5-channels starts abruptly with the code panel and `falloff=0 radius=0`, which hides the visual the viewer just learned. Add "A light can also be switched off for a mesh." as the first sentence. c5-shadows comes after channels and its opening ("Simple point lights cast no shadows") is a bit of a non-sequitur; consider moving it before channels (record -> radius -> no shadows -> channels), then end the chapter on a closing sentence that hands to the specular term of chapter 6 (that is currently done by chapter 6's first sentence only).
7. **Depth.** The chapter never says what the light does with the result (it goes to diffuse and specular, chapter 6/7), and never says what "intensity in candelas" means for a viewer who only knows lux or lumens. The `lightChannels` GI flag is mentioned in the struct sentence and never again.

## Chapter 6 · GGX specular

**Fit.** Tight: 130 s of natural narration in 136 s (1.05x), only about 6 s of pause. Many sentences carry 2-5 s orbit animations that must finish inside the sentence; with so little slack, the viewer gets no breathing room, and animations that outlast their sentence get cut by the next cue. Raise the budget to about 155 s, or trim (see 6). The panel is not cut off, nothing overlaps; the formula panel is very full but legible.

1. **c6-terms: F shows no visible change.** The F frame (`viewMode=5`) is a flat cream disc on black: Fresnel hardly varies over a sphere lit at one angle, and the 25 degree light orbit changes nothing visible. The sentence says "reflectance rises toward white as the light and the view graze the microfacet".
   - Show it with a sweep over H·V, not light position: sweep `lightY` down toward the horizon so `HdotV` falls from 0.70 to 0.1 (it does in c6-sweep, but only there). Or show F as a plot of F against H·V with a marker. Light the readout row `HdotV` and `F` (`highlight` on the rows) with the sentence.
   - Light-orbit directions in c6-terms (+/-20 degrees, 2.7 s, 3.5 s) do not make the point and cost time; do them with a clear purpose or remove (see 5).
2. **c6-terms: D shows 2.49 where the sentence says "far above one".** The readout reads `D 2.4881` at the start. For roughnessA2 = 0.0171 the peak is 1/(pi * 0.0171) = 18.6. The viewer sees 2.49 and hears "far above one".
   - Make the orbit pass through the peak (light on the mirror direction, N·H = 1). In c6-sweep the readout reaches D = 14.75, which is better; so sweep to peak inside c6-terms, or move the claim there.
   - Say what D integrates: "it is a density: the fraction of facets facing H, per solid angle". The current sentence says "the density of microfacet normals", which is right but not intuitive. A viewer who knows graphics knows D; but they do not know why it blows up for smooth surfaces. One clause: "the smoother the surface, the more normals bunch around N, so D at the peak is about one over pi alpha squared".
   - False colour has no scale or legend (it is a scalar in viridis-like colours); say "bright means large" and add a colour bar if the lab supports it.
3. **c6-terms: G beat's false colour shows a sharp half-sphere cut (yellow/dark) that is unexplained.** That edge is the terminator of N·L (G's L term goes to 0 there, or the light is behind it). The narration says nothing about it, and a viewer will wonder. Add: "The dark half faces away from the light, where N dot L is zero". Also "O3DE returns it already divided by four N dot L and N dot V": that is a statement about code, with no step explaining why (V = G / (4 N·L N·V), so f = D V F N·L). One sentence of the form "so the product is just D times V times F times N dot L" and the product formula would make that tangible; the formula panel already holds `G = 0.5/(Lambda_V + Lambda_L)`. Light the `0.5/(...)` form while saying it. Also "multiscatter compensation", named in sentence 1, is never explained here (it is chapter 9): add "(more on that compensation in chapter nine)" or drop it from this sentence.
4. **c6-vectors: first sentence bridge and the V explanation.**
   - The chapter's first sentence is good ("what the surface sends back of it is the BRDF").
   - N, L, V, H each get a 0.6 s blend and one clause; the labels are tiny (frames 2-3) and the arrows overlap near the point (N, H, V cluster within a few pixels). Highlight each vector as it is named: scale up its label and dim the others (`vectorsShown` already reveals them one at a time; add a pulse/thickness cue).
   - "so that all four lie in a plane facing us": show the plane (a translucent quad) or draw the 2D diagram view. The camera moves to yaw=0 at that sentence, which is the right move; keep it, but the viewer cannot see the plane claim.
   - The H sentence ("a microfacet whose normal points along H mirrors the light straight into the viewer") needs a visual: draw the microfacet as a small tilted tile at the point, aligned with H, with the reflected ray from L to V. The two +/-25 degree orbits only shake the light, not the microfacet. Show the facet and let H align with it; as the light moves, H and the facet move together, N does not.
   - Also say why only H matters: "only facets facing H reflect L into V; D measures how many there are".
5. **c6-sweep: most of the movements are not tied to the readout.**
   - "The pink curve is the specular response for every outgoing direction in this plane, and it leans around the mirror direction of L": the orbit moves 12 degrees both ways in 7 s. Say what to watch: the mirror direction arrow. Draw the mirror direction R of L as a dashed ray, so "leans around the mirror direction" has something to lean around.
   - "Raise the light toward the normal and the lobe stands up with it", then "Lower it toward the horizon: N dot L falls, Fresnel grows, and the lobe stretches out." These are the best sentences in the chapter, and they have the right sweeps; light the changing rows (`NdotL`, `F`, `D`) and keep the sentence aligned with the sweep. The 50 degree sweep in 4.5 s is quick for four claims; stretch to 7 s.
   - Last sentence "The readout follows every term as the light moves" is filler with two orbits; cut it, or replace it with the key observation: the product `D·G·F·N·L` is large only near the mirror direction.
6. **c6-rough is fine but short.** The sweep `sphereRoughness` 0.15 -> 0.7 is correct. Add the readout: D peak (`D` row) falls from about 15 to 0.8, and the highlight on the sphere visibly broadens. The false-colour frame shows a pinpoint; end on the broad glow. Say roughness squared: "alpha is roughness squared" since the formula uses `alpha^2` and the readout shows `roughnessA2` (0.0171 vs 0.0026, which are two different values at 0.35 and 0.15); define `roughnessA2` as it is lit in the panel.
7. **Trim to give the chapter air.** Remove the c6-sweep final sentence, the F orbit in c6-terms, and the redundant "In false colour, D is bright only where H meets the normal" orbit (D already lit). That frees about 10 s, and the chapter reaches the 1.2x ratio.
8. **Flow.** Chapter 5's last beat is sources; chapter 6 opens fine. Between c6-vectors and c6-terms, the camera changes from 3D to the observer's view with no sentence saying so; add "From the same point of view, here are the three terms" or hold the scene. c6-terms to c6-sweep: "Now the numbers at the point, and its lobe" is a good bridge. The chapter ends on roughness, and it does not hand off to the diffuse half (chapter 7); add a last sentence in c6-rough: "That is the specular half; the diffuse half is next."
